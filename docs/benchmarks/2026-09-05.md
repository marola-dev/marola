# Ocean-answer benchmark — 2026-09-05 snapshot

`just benchmark` (`cli/bench/OceanBenchmark`, 22 questions: 10 inside the `knowledge/` corpus, 12
outside — science, history, animals, nature), three arms on the same local `llama3.2`:
`baseline` = the plain prompt, `rag-strict` = grounded-or-abstain, `rag-general` = grounded, else a
labelled general-knowledge answer (the default `--ask` mode). Coverage = share of expected keywords
present; cited = a `[n]` citation present; abstained = "no notes on that".

Kept in the repo as the reference point for "is marola better than just asking the model, and by
how much" — re-run and compare before changing the prompt, the corpus, the embedder or the model.

## Run 1 — before the no-answer sentinel (`llama3.2` embeddings, absolute threshold 0.3)

| arm | coverage (in-corpus) | coverage (general) | coverage (all) | cited | abstained | mean ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 0.57 | 1.00 | 0.80 | 0% | 0% | 444 |
| rag-strict | 0.90 | 0.04 | 0.43 | 50% | 0% | 692 |
| rag-general | 0.87 | 0.08 | 0.44 | 50% | 0% | 429 |

What it showed: inside the corpus RAG wins clearly (0.90 vs 0.57, with citations); outside it
collapses (0.04) *and the general fallback never fired* — `llama3.2`'s own embeddings scored
irrelevant passages 0.49-0.50 and relevant ones 0.36-0.40, so an absolute cosine threshold cannot
separate them. The model was forced to answer from unrelated passages and said so in prose, which
the abstain detector also missed.

## Run 2 — with the sentinel (`llama3.2` embeddings, threshold off)

The grounded prompt now asks the model to reply `NO_ANSWER_IN_PASSAGES` when the passages don't
cover the question; `OceanQa` then falls back (general) or abstains (strict). Threshold default 0.

| arm | coverage (in-corpus) | coverage (general) | coverage (all) | cited | abstained | mean ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 0.55 | 0.92 | 0.75 | 0% | 0% | 514 |
| rag-strict | 0.73 | 0.03 | 0.35 | 32% | 59% | 329 |
| rag-general | 0.92 | 0.78 | 0.84 | 41% | 0% | 608 |

**This is the result that matters: `rag-general` beats the plain prompt overall (0.84 vs 0.75)
while citing on 41% of answers; the plain prompt cites on none.** Inside the corpus 0.92 vs 0.55.
Strict mode abstains on 59% of all questions — honest, and the worse product.

## Run 3 — with the sentinel, `nomic-embed-text` embeddings

| arm | coverage (in-corpus) | coverage (general) | coverage (all) | cited | abstained | mean ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 0.57 | 1.00 | 0.80 | 0% | 0% | 466 |
| rag-strict | 0.85 | 0.03 | 0.40 | 32% | 45% | 366 |
| rag-general | 0.85 | 0.67 | 0.75 | 41% | 0% | 456 |

Not clearly better than run 2 on 22 questions: in-corpus 0.85 vs 0.92, general 0.67 vs 0.78. Two
honest notes: (1) the model's answers are non-deterministic, so ±0.1 between runs is noise at this
sample size (the *baseline* moved 0.75→0.80 between runs 2 and 3 with no change); (2) once the
model decides relevance itself via the sentinel, the embedder mostly affects *which* passages it
sees, and with a six-document corpus the right one is usually in the top four either way. A
sharper embedder will matter when the corpus is ten times larger.

## How to beat the baseline further (from the verdict, in order)

1. Add corpus documents for the topics where strict abstained — science, history, animals,
   nature — one `knowledge/*.md` per topic with a `Source:` line. Each turns an unsourced answer
   into a cited one; that is the only lever that raises "cited" above ~40%.
2. Keep `general` as the default mode: a labelled unsourced answer beats a refusal for the person
   asking; the label is the honesty.
3. Two to four sentences, source line always visible (the bot, MIP-0002, must render it).
4. Grow the question set with the questions real users ask (MIP-0002's counters), and add an
   LLM-as-judge score alongside keyword coverage once there is an eval set (`FUTURE-WORK.md` §4.1).
5. Re-run this benchmark before merging any change to the prompt, corpus, embedder or model, and
   compare against this file.
