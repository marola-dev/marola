# Bathing-water quality (balneabilidade) in Brazil and Santa Catarina
Source: https://balneabilidade.ima.sc.gov.br/

In Brazil, whether a beach's water is fit for bathing is classified under CONAMA Resolution 274 of 2000. Agencies take water samples at fixed points along each beach and count faecal-indicator bacteria, mainly enterococci. A point is PRÓPRIA (fit) when at least 80% of its last five weekly samples have 100 enterococci or fewer per 100 mL of water (or 800 E. coli or fewer). Otherwise it is IMPRÓPRIA (unfit). The classification is per sampling point, not per beach: a stream mouth can be unfit while the sand 300 metres away is fine.

In Santa Catarina the monitoring is run by IMA, the state environment institute, at about 260 points across 28 coastal municipalities. Results are published in weekly bulletins and on an online map. During the bathing season (roughly October to April) points are sampled every week. Off-season, from April to September, most of the coast is sampled once a month, in the last week of the month, so a result can be up to five weeks old; only Balneário Camboriú's central beach stays weekly year-round.

Why points near stream and river mouths are so often IMPRÓPRIA: they carry whatever drains from the neighbourhood behind the beach — untreated sewage, storm runoff, animal waste. Heavy rain in the previous 24 to 48 hours pushes contamination out to sea and is the single biggest reason for a bad result, which is why bulletins record the rainfall at sampling time. After a storm, avoid swimming near any outflow even if the last bulletin said PRÓPRIA.

What the numbers mean for a swimmer: enterococci themselves rarely make you ill; they indicate faecal contamination, and with it the possibility of the viruses and bacteria that cause gastroenteritis, ear and skin infections. Risk rises with the count and with swallowing water. Children, older people and anyone immunocompromised are more susceptible.

How marola uses this: it matches the agency's sampling points to each beach, shows the latest classification, sample date and count per point, and applies the agency's own PRÓPRIA/IMPRÓPRIA classification rather than re-deriving it. If every fresh point on a beach is IMPRÓPRIA the beach scores zero; if only some are, the score drops and the spots to avoid are named; a sample older than 45 days is treated as no data and said so.
